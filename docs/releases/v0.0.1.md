grace9000's first prototype: a bare, modular photo editor, and the sister
of splice9000 and wave9000. Mac only for now.

## What's in it

- **Steps**, run top to bottom and never written into the photo: Exposure,
  White Balance, Contrast, Highlights & Shadows, Levels, Saturation (with
  vibrance), Curve, Black & White, Vignette. Drag to reorder, turn any off,
  compare with the original (`\`).
- **Crop and straighten** (`c`): edges, corners, shapes from 1:1 to 16:9,
  straightening that never leaves an empty corner, quarter turns and
  mirrors.
- **A preview made at the size it's shown**, on its own thread, from a
  pyramid of the photo, with a live histogram. Zoom to the photo's own
  pixels and move around.
- **Export** to JPEG, PNG (8 or 16 bits), WebP and TIFF, shrunk to a long
  edge if you like. PNG needs no ffmpeg.
- **`.grace` files**: the edit as readable JSON beside the photo. The edit
  in progress survives quitting and crashes.
- **The family's layout, keys and adapter host**: panels go anywhere,
  every command can be re-keyed in `keys.toml`, and adapters can add
  commands, panels, export formats and their own steps (`image.render`
  gives them the finished picture).

## Measured

Release build on an M-series MacBook Air:

- 6.5 MB program (Apple Silicon); the universal download is a 7.1 MB zip
- 55 MB fresh; 98 MB with a 6 MP photo; 184 MB with a 24 MP JPEG
- 0.0% CPU when idle
- Preview at 1470×916: 10 ms plain, 33 ms with six steps, straightened
  and cropped

## Not yet

Color management (photos are treated as sRGB), camera raw, brushes and
masks, sharpening and noise reduction, metadata in exports, more than one
photo at a time, Windows and Linux downloads.

## Installing

Unzip, move grace9000.app to Applications, and right-click → Open the
first time (it isn't notarized). `brew install ffmpeg` to open anything
other than PNG.
